# Order Lifecycle

Six states, from the brief's Required Features tab. `[brief]`

| # | State | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Ordered** | Triggered at admission or pre-admission. Hospice specifies patient, equipment, urgency. |
| 02 | **Dispatched** | Vendor assigns to a route. ETA generated. |
| 03 | **In Transit / At Risk** | Live status. **A risk signal should fire here if delivery won't beat a deadline.** |
| 04 | **Delivered** | Proof of delivery captured. Hospice and family notified. |
| 05 | **Pickup Triggered** | Patient status change (death, discharge) flags equipment for retrieval. |
| 06 | **Pickup Delayed** | Retrieval hasn't happened in the expected window. Family is still looking at equipment they don't need. |

## Required features by side `[brief]`

### Hospice side (table stakes)
- Patient and equipment need: type, quantity, urgency, target date
- **Discharge-readiness flag** — equipment confirmed before a scheduled discharge
- **Post-death pickup trigger**, ideally tied to an EMR status change rather than a manual call
- **Vendor choice within a market** — most hospices work multiple vendors, not one
- **Total cost-of-care visibility** — DME spend alongside medication spend, not siloed
- Mobile and tablet-friendly ordering at the bedside

### Vendor side (marked "Hardest part" — where they most want originality)
- Fleet/route capacity, service area, current load
- Serialized equipment inventory: in stock, out, overdue for pickup
- Delivery and pickup status with proof-of-capture (signature, photo, timestamp)
- SLA and contract terms per hospice client, tracked against actual performance
- Resupply cadence for consumables (CPAP supplies, wound care) tied to payer-approved timelines
- Billing trigger tied to delivery completion (denial rates 15–25%, mostly documentation gaps)
- Vendor recruitment and onboarding from a cold start

> Note the tension: the brief lists vendor recruitment as a required feature, but the FAQ later says network-building is **out of scope** and vendor participation should be treated as given. **The FAQ is newer and more specific — follow the FAQ.** `[faq]`

### Shared / notification layer — where the "Differentiator" tags are
- **Real-time status visible to both sides**, not just inside the vendor's system — *Differentiator*
- **Service-failure risk scoring**, surfacing an at-risk order **before** it's late — *Differentiator*
- Escalation path to a case manager or vendor rep when a risk threshold is crossed
- **Explainability**: "why was this order flagged at-risk?" needs a legible answer, not a black box

## The two risk states are the product

DME-10305 and DME-09803 are the two failure modes the whole bounty exists to fix. If the demo doesn't visibly handle both, it hasn't addressed the core user problems (25% of the rubric). `[assumed]`
