# Vendor Value Prop — how we win the party that never logs in

Decision context 2026-08-14. `[team]` synthesis grounded in `[research]` facts. Vin's framing: win vendors by making their lives easier or making them money. Patterns borrowed from two verified Utah companies.

## The two patterns

**Redo** (Draper; $81M Series B at $1.25B, June 2026; returns platform): won merchants by being **free and zero-effort for the party they needed** — no SaaS fee, monetized elsewhere ($1–2 shopper coverage). Took the painful afterthought (returns) and made it a first-class product. `[research]`
→ Our translation: vendor pays nothing, installs nothing, logs into nothing. Reply to a text = onboarded. The hospice pays PPD (already settled `[faq]`). Pickup/returns get first-class lifecycle status, not afterthought status.
→ **Precision (deeper research 2026-08-14):** Redo never operated physical reverse logistics — they won on *payer-shifting* (shopper pays, merchant free) and *exchange-first UX* (return becomes a second sale). Don't claim we copy their logistics playbook; there isn't one. The borrow only works because **we name our payer**: the hospice's PPD funds the platform, and the vendor's payback is saved truck rolls, faster asset recovery, and denial-proof documentation. Origin note: founder Tay Brown built it solo cold-calling merchants; the Divvy team joined later to scale — the wedge was won on the free-and-effortless promise, not brand. `[research]`

**Plaibook** (Utah; AI texting engine born on a pest-control sales floor, now dozens of home-service ops): the AI does the SMB's communication labor; each interaction's data compounds into the next feature. `[research]`
→ Our translation: the agent does the dispatcher's phone-tag — coordinates pickup windows with the family, answers "where is it" calls, runs ETA nudges. Every text/GPS ping/photo compounds into the reliability score and ETA model.

## The four vendor wins

1. **Zero adoption cost.** No login, no fee, no training, no IT. Magic link + SMS only. (Redo pattern; matches FAQ baseline persona.)
2. **We do their phone-tag.** Agent handles family scheduling and status calls that currently interrupt the dispatcher. (Plaibook pattern.)
3. **We make them money.** Auto-generated proof pack (timestamp + signature + photo + geocode) attacks the 15–25% DME claim-denial rate (mostly documentation gaps `[landscape]`) and triggers billing at delivery confirmation. Denial-proof documentation is cash.
4. **Reliability is a revenue asset, not a stick.** The score routes order volume: good vendors visibly win more orders in the compare view. Marketplace flywheel, not compliance report. Plus: pickup requests arrive as batchable windows that respect the ~$100–200 unpaid truck roll ([[reverse-logistics-and-pickup]]) instead of demanding instant runs.

## Cross-industry precedents (verified, for pitch + Q&A)

- **CHEP** (pallet pooling, hundreds of millions of units): ML ranks which unreturned pallets are recoverable and hands agents a **weekly worked call-list** — CHEP reports +500k pallets/yr recovered in Europe (their number; say "CHEP reports"). A hospital bed in a home is a rented pooled asset with worse tracking than a wooden pallet. → Feature echo: Pickup Delayed items form a **worked recovery queue**, not a dashboard. `[research]`
- **Frontdoor/American Home Shield** (public co): dispatches ~24,000 independent contractors, 85% small shops, **ranks every one on outcomes**, rewards with "preferred contractor" status. The reliability flywheel proven at scale on exactly our vendor profile. `[research]`
- **Happy Returns/UPS**: retail made the return a product, not an exception — 10,000 box-free drop-off points with instant confirmation at custody transfer. Best pitch *opener* for a non-logistics room (but the drop-off model inverts for DME — don't overextend it). `[research]`
- **MicroStar** (1.4M kegs): pay-per-fill pooling — brewer never chases the asset. Blue Rhino: **swap, don't retrieve** — matches our oxygen-swap stop. `[research]`

## Pitch line

"Every other tool asks the vendor to adopt software. We ask them to answer a text — and in exchange we schedule their pickups, document their deliveries so claims don't bounce, and send them more business when they're good."
